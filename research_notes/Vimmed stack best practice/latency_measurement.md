# Input-to-display latency for editors and terminals (Wayland, macOS): targets and measurement

Scope note: sources range from 2015 (Fatin) to 2026. Classic measurement articles are older and are flagged as such. Citation lines use the form "claim ([Source](URL))". Anything not backed by a fetched source this session sits under Inferences or Gaps and is labelled.

## 1. Measured end-to-end latency of popular editors and terminals

### Takeaway
Credible end-to-end (key to light) numbers for good editors and terminals on 60 Hz displays cluster around 30 to 36 ms average with hardware testers (Tristan Hume, macOS, 2020; Dan Luu, macOS, about 2017), while software-only tools like Typometer report much smaller "processing" numbers (1 to 30 ms) because they exclude the keyboard, compositor scan-out and panel. The two kinds of numbers are not comparable and are routinely mixed up in blogs, especially 2025 to 2026 Ghostty and Zed reviews.

### Cited Findings
**Hardware-tester (key to light) results**
- Tristan Hume (May 2020), Teensy LC plus light sensor, 1000 Hz USB polling, 60 Hz displays on macOS. Averages: Sublime Text 32.5 ms, TextEdit 33.4, macOS Emacs 33.2, Chrome text input 34.2, Xi 35.0, Atom 45.6, VS Code 47.6; Apple Terminal 35.8 (35.1 running vim), kitty 36.1, Alacritty 50.4, iTerm2 50.5 (53.1 with GPU rendering) ([Hume, Making a latency tester](https://thume.ca/2020/05/20/making-a-latency-tester/)).
- Hume: the top and bottom of the screen differed by about 16 ms because of row scan-out, and two monitors differed by about 30 ms, "nearly half of the end-to-end latency in the Sublime test"; most editors were "basically as good as you can get" ([Hume](https://thume.ca/2020/05/20/making-a-latency-tester/)). He added 50 ms random jitter between samples late in the project so samples would not lock to refresh; earlier results have artificially low variance (same source).
- Dan Luu (undated, about 2017, mid-2014 MacBook Pro, macOS 10.12), keypress to internal screen capture, 10k presses per measurement, p50 / p99.9 idle: Terminal.app 6 / 25 ms, emacs-eshell 5 / 17, emacs-term 13 / 28, st 25 / 63, alacritty 31 / 36, hyper 32 / 49, iTerm2 44 / 60. Under load (Rust compile) tails got much worse, e.g. st p99.9 111 ms, alacritty 56 ([Dan Luu, Terminal latency](https://danluu.com/term-latency/)). Note: internal screen capture excludes panel latency, so these sit between Typometer-style and photodiode numbers.
- Ivan Molodetskikh (6 April 2024), Teensy plus light sensor (after Hume's design, own firmware), 120 repetitions per test, about 35,500 sensor readings/s, randomised waits, 144 Hz 2560x1440 monitor, raw Mutter 46.0 without GNOME Shell. VTE (GNOME Terminal, Console, VTE test app) went from "quite bad" in GNOME 45 to "pretty much on par with Alacritty" in GNOME 46. The article gives plots, not numeric medians. The spread of measurements roughly equals one refresh cycle (about 7 ms at 144 Hz). The main fix was moving VTE "away from a 40 Hz VTE repaint timer to drawing every frame, synchronized with the monitor" ([bxt.rs, Just How Much Faster Are the GNOME 46 Terminals?](https://bxt.rs/blog/just-how-much-faster-are-the-gnome-46-terminals/)).
- Later Molodetskikh social posts (undated in results, mirrored): on a vtebench complex-drawing test foot was "firmly ahead, followed by Kitty and Alacritty"; in another test "Kitty loses one refresh cycle for some reason" ([grishka.me mirror](https://friends.grishka.me/posts/1091768), [Mastodon](https://mastodon.online/users/YaLTeR/statuses/110848066454900941)). Mirrors only; original figures not verified.
- GNOME VTE issue (2020 to 2021): VTE-based terminals had about 20 ms more input latency than non-VTE GTK3 apps and non-VTE terminals ([GNOME VTE #334](https://gitlab.gnome.org/GNOME/vte/-/issues/334)). Superseded by the GNOME 46 fix above.
- kitty docs: hardware measurements on macOS put kitty and Terminal.app at the top; Typometer on Linux put kitty best "by a wide margin"; no millisecond figures given ([kitty Performance](https://sw.kovidgoyal.net/kitty/performance/)). anarc.at (2022) notes kitty's developers quoted 35 ms with a hardware tester versus 7 ms with Typometer for the same terminal ([anarc.at, Wayland terminal emulators](https://anarc.at/blog/2022-09-19-wayland-terminal-emulators/)). This 5x gap is the clearest illustration of hardware vs software measurement.

**Typometer (software, processing-only) results**
- Pavel Fatin (December 2015), 1920x1080 at 59.94 Hz, i5-3427U, Windows 7 Classic theme (no Aero) and Lubuntu 15.10. Windows averages: GVim 0.9 ms, IDEA zero-latency 2.9, Notepad++ 4.3, Emacs 5.3, Sublime 8.2, Eclipse 10.1, NetBeans 11.8, IDEA 24.7, Atom 49.4. Linux (XML file): IDEA zero-latency 1.7, GVim 4.5, Gedit 12.4, Emacs 20.3, Sublime 23.1, Atom 32.5, IDEA 69.2 ([Fatin, Typing with pleasure](https://pavelfatin.com/typing-with-pleasure/)).
- Fatin estimates typical keyboard latency at 8 to 22 ms (average 14) and monitor at about 12 ms average, so about 26 ms of the end-to-end budget is outside the application; he suggests 1 to 5 ms as the target for an editor's own painting and calls 16 ms "quite arbitrary". Aero compositing adds at least one frame ([Fatin](https://pavelfatin.com/typing-with-pleasure/)).
- LWN (Antoine Beaupré, April 2018), Typometer on X11 (Wayland could not be tested because Typometer needs to inject keys and read other windows): uxterm 1.7 ms mean, mlterm 1.8, Vim Athena 2.8, Vim GTK2 3.9, Emacs 4.8, gedit 8.9, Konsole 13.4, Alacritty 15.1, st 15.7, Vim GTK3 16.5, urxvt 18.3, GNOME Terminal 27.1, Xfce Terminal 27.4. GNOME (Mutter, compositing) added about 20 ms versus i3; Fedora GNOME on Xorg pushed VTE terminals near 50 ms ([LWN, A look at terminal emulators part 2](https://lwn.net/Articles/751763/)).

**Ghostty and Zed**
- No primary, reproducible latency measurement for Ghostty was found. A Typometer-based write-up puts Ghostty at about 13 ms average, behind xterm 3.5, Alacritty 4.2 to 6.9 and tuned kitty about 6.5; 2026 review blogs claim 1.2 to 2 ms with unstated method, an order of magnitude disagreement ([cboone macOS terminal report](https://github.fleetingtimer.workers.dev/cboone/reports/blob/main/terminal/macos-terminal-emulators.md), [DevToolReviews](https://www.devtoolreviews.com/reviews/ghostty-vs-iterm2-vs-kitty)). Treat all as unreliable.
- Zed: third-party blogs cite about 2 ms "keystroke to glyph" for Zed versus about 12 ms for VS Code and Cursor; methodology not stated and possibly a shared source ([Vantaige](https://vantaige.io/blog/zed-1-vs-cursor-vs-vscode-real-benchmark-2026), [falcao.org](https://falcao.org/posts/zed-1-0/)). These are processing numbers at best, not glass.

### Inferences
- Typical at 60 Hz, end to end with a photodiode: about 30 to 36 ms for the best apps on a composited desktop (macOS 2020 data), 45 to 50+ ms for slower ones. No equivalent clean Linux Wayland 60 Hz table was found, but the foot spike's own on-glass p50 of 28 to 31 ms (presentation-feedback, which excludes the keyboard and the panel) is consistent with being in the "as good as it gets" band.
- Typical at 120 to 144 Hz: Molodetskikh's spread of about one refresh (about 7 ms at 144 Hz) implies the refresh-dependent portion shrinks proportionally. No credible absolute 120 Hz glass number for editors was found.
- vimmed's hand-off p95 of 5 ms (raw Wayland) is in the Fatin "1 to 5 ms" band and comparable to the best Typometer results. The GTK4 shell's p95 of 14 ms is in the Konsole/Alacritty-2018 band: acceptable, but it costs a frame some of the time at 60 Hz.

### Gaps
- No primary-source numbers for neovim in foot, kitty, Alacritty, Ghostty, VS Code, Zed or Sublime on Wayland with a hardware tester. Molodetskikh's later multi-terminal posts were only available via mirrors.
- Zed's and Ghostty's own official latency claims were not found as primary pages this session.
- Dan Luu's keyboard latency article (keyboards range widely) was not fetched; Fatin's 8 to 22 ms estimate stands in.

## 2. Compositor pipeline on Wayland

### Takeaway
Every vsynced compositor adds a wait for its next repaint plus at least the scan-out of the next frame; the only knobs that reduce this are compositing late in the frame (Sway `max_render_time`), direct scanout, and tearing (fullscreen only). Triple buffering in Mutter and Hyprland is dynamic and is claimed to add no latency when frames are on time.

### Cited Findings
- Sway `max_render_time`: "Controls when sway composites the output, as a positive number of milliseconds before the next display refresh." Smaller values give fresher frames and lower input latency; too small and the refresh is missed. `off` composites right after a refresh (maximum render time, maximum latency) ([sway-output(5)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/swaywm/sway/master/sway/sway-output.5.scd)).
- Sway `allow_tearing`: immediate page flips; recommended with `max_render_time off`; only effective when a window is fullscreen and both output and application allow it ([sway-output(5)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/swaywm/sway/master/sway/sway-output.5.scd)).
- Mutter dynamic triple buffering (MR !1441) was merged on 14 February 2025 for GNOME 48 after five years of review, led by Daniel van Vugt (Canonical); it switches to a third buffer only when the previous frame runs late and otherwise stays double buffered ([Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/GNOME-48-Triple-Buffering)). Ubuntu's debrief describes it as keeping rendering one frame ahead of scan-out only when the system cannot keep up ([Ubuntu Discourse, Triple Buffering a debrief](https://discourse.ubuntu.com/t/triple-buffering-a-debrief/56314)).
- Hyprland 0.50 (July 2025) added `render:new_render_scheduling`, which dynamically switches to triple buffering when the system cannot keep up, "with no performance or latency cost when the system is doing alright"; experimental, off by default, intended for default in 0.51. It also removed the render-ahead-of-time settings ("nuked") and the `explicit_sync` settings (explicit sync always on) ([Hyprland 0.50 news](https://hypr.land/news/update50/), [Phoronix](https://phoronix.com/news/Hyprland-0.50-Released)).
- LWN 2018: Mutter compositing added about 20 ms versus i3 on X11 ([LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/751763/)). Old, X11, pre-dates Mutter's 2020s frame-clock work.
- fifo-v1 and commit-timing-v1 landed as staging protocols in wayland-protocols 1.38 ([Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Wayland-Protocols-1.38), [MR 248](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/wayland/wayland-protocols/-/merge_requests/248)). Mesa 24.3 uses them for Vulkan FIFO on Wayland ([Phoronix](https://phoronix.com/news/Mesa-24.3-Vulkan-FIFO)); Mutter merged support in January 2025 for GNOME 48 ([Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/GNOME-Mutter-Wayland-Timing)); KWin has fifo-v1 since Plasma 6.4 per a KDE bug commenter, commit-timing status unclear ([KDE bug 491219](https://bugs.kde.org/show_bug.cgi?id=491219)). These protocols let a client hold an update until a target time: they help pacing (video, games), not keystroke latency.
- wp_presentation `presented`: the time the update "turned into light the first time on the surface's main output"; compositors may estimate it from flip completion adjusted for known display-path latency. Flags tell you how trustworthy it is: `vsync`, `hw_clock` (hardware-provided timestamp), `hw_completion` (hardware signalled scan-out start, not a timer guess), `zero_copy` (direct scanout or overlay). `refresh` gives the period; `seq` the MSC; `discarded` means never shown. The clock is a `clockid_t` sent at bind, and "applies only to presentation timestamps, not to core input event timestamps" ([wayland.app, presentation-time](https://wayland.app/protocols/presentation-time)).
- foot: `delayed-render-lower` (default 0.5 ms) and `delayed-render-upper` (default about 8.3 ms) delay rendering after client (pty) data to reduce flicker; 0 disables and renders "as soon as possible". `render-timer` prints per-frame render time. foot relies on the compositor releasing the previous buffer immediately after a new frame ([foot.ini(5)](https://codeberg.org/dnkl/foot/raw/branch/master/doc/foot.ini.5.scd)). Its performance wiki covers damage tracking but not input latency ([foot wiki, Performance](https://codeberg.org/dnkl/foot/wiki/Performance)).
- kitty: `input_delay` (3 ms default per its macOS measurements) and `repaint_delay` are artificial render-loop delays to save CPU; low-latency recipe is `input_delay 0`, `repaint_delay 2`, `sync_to_monitor no`, `wayland_enable_ime no` ([kitty Performance](https://sw.kovidgoyal.net/kitty/performance/)).
- VTE's big 2024 latency win was replacing a fixed 40 Hz timer with per-frame drawing synced to the monitor (GTK frame clock) ([bxt.rs](https://bxt.rs/blog/just-how-much-faster-are-the-gnome-46-terminals/)).

### Inferences
- Floor arithmetic for a key at a random phase on a 60 Hz vsynced compositor (T = 16.7 ms): the client commit waits for the compositor's next repaint (uniform 0 to T), the compositor then composites and flips at a vblank, and wp_presentation `presented` marks the start of scan-out. If the compositor composites right after vblank and flips at the next one (Sway `max_render_time off` behaviour), commit to presented is roughly uniform on [T, 2T]: mean about 25 ms, p95 about 32 ms. Add the app's own hand-off time. The spike's raw-Wayland p50 28 to 31 ms and p95 40 ms (hand-off p95 5 ms) fit this model plus a few ms, with the p95 tail suggesting occasional extra frame (missed latch or Hyprland scheduling). With late compositing (`max_render_time` near the composite cost), commit to presented approaches uniform [0, T] plus composite time: mean about 10 ms, p95 about 17 to 18 ms at 60 Hz.
- Consequence: "key to glass p95 <= 16 ms at 60 Hz" is unattainable for a composited, vsynced client even with a zero-cost app and an ideal late-compositing compositor, because the random-phase wait alone has p95 about 0.95 T = 15.8 ms. The spike's redefinition is correct.
- Why GTK4 hand-off is 14 ms p95 but glass equals raw Wayland: GTK's frame clock waits for the frame callback before drawing, so it spends the wait inside the app that raw Wayland spends inside the compositor queue. The total is the same, which is why on-glass p50/p95 match. This means hand-off time alone can mislead: a client that commits immediately looks great on hand-off but its commit simply sits in the compositor. Hand-off must therefore be reported with on-glass.
- Committing immediately on key (no frame-callback wait) only beats frame-callback pacing if the compositor latches late (Sway with `max_render_time`, or Mutter/KWin with their own late-latch scheduling). On a compositor that composites early, both approaches land on the same vblank.
- To find the true floor on the test laptop, rerun the raw shell under Sway with `max_render_time` at 1 to 3 ms, and optionally fullscreen with `allow_tearing yes` plus the tearing-control protocol, as a lower bound.

### Gaps
- The Hyprland wiki variables page (direct_scanout, vrr, vfr, allow_tearing exact wording) returned 404 during this session; current wording unverified.
- No primary source found on how Hyprland or KWin schedule composite timing relative to vblank (early vs late latch) in 2025 to 2026, nor measured compositor-added latency per compositor on the same hardware.
- foot's historical `presentation-timings` tweak option and blog notes on frame callbacks were not found in the current foot.ini(5); it may have been removed. Needs a check of foot's CHANGELOG.

## 3. macOS composition latency at 60/120 Hz and ProMotion

### Takeaway
No credible measurement of keystroke-to-photon on Apple Silicon ProMotion was found. The best macOS data (60 Hz, 2020) puts the fastest editors at about 33 ms end to end, which means everything after the app (WindowServer, scan-out, panel, keyboard) accounts for most of the budget.

### Cited Findings
- WindowServer is the macOS display compositor and input event router; keyboard events pass through it on the way to apps ([Eclectic Light](https://eclecticlight.co/2020/06/08/windowserver-display-compositor-and-input-event-router/)).
- macOS 60 Hz hardware measurements: Sublime 32.5 ms, Terminal.app 35.8, kitty 36.1 average key to light ([Hume](https://thume.ca/2020/05/20/making-a-latency-tester/)). Terminal.app p50 6 ms idle by internal screen capture ([Dan Luu](https://danluu.com/term-latency/)).
- Consumer coverage says ProMotion mostly improves scrolling and animation on the Mac; lower input latency claims are mainly about Apple Pencil on iPad ([Digital Citizen](https://www.digitalcitizen.life/what-is-apple-promotion-display/)). Secondary source only.

### Inferences
- The spike's 4.4 ms p95 to CATransaction flush is a hand-off figure, equivalent to the Wayland "frame handed to compositor" number, not glass. Comparing it with the Wayland on-glass 40 ms is apples to oranges.
- With ProMotion, macOS drops to low refresh rates when idle; the first keystroke after idle may be presented at a lower rate or incur a ramp-up. This should be tested rather than assumed (no source found).
- For macOS glass timing, `CAMetalLayer`/`MTLDrawable` `presentedTime` (Metal `addPresentedHandler`) or `CADisplayLink` timestamps are the analogue of wp_presentation. Not verified this session.

### Gaps
- No Apple documentation or measurement on WindowServer added latency per refresh rate, nor on ProMotion behaviour for keyboard text input.

## 4. Measurement methodology

### Takeaway
Credible numbers need a stated start point and end point. The gold standard is a microcontroller that both sends a USB HID key and reads a photodiode on the glyph region (Hume, Molodetskikh). Software-only methods are fine for regression tracking if every timestamp is on one monotonic clock and the input enters at the kernel (uinput) rather than via a Wayland virtual-keyboard tool.

### Cited Findings
- Typometer generates OS input events and uses screen capture to time the change; it excludes keyboard, USB, GPU and monitor latency (Fatin estimates over 20 ms excluded in typical setups) and cannot work on Wayland because it needs to inject keys into and read other windows ([LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/751763/), [Fatin](https://pavelfatin.com/typing-with-pleasure/), [anarc.at](https://anarc.at/blog/2022-09-19-wayland-terminal-emulators/)).
- Hardware tester design: Teensy LC (about USD 12) plus light sensor module (about USD 12) plus a helping hand, 1000 Hz USB polling avoids the about 8 ms extra of 125 Hz devices; firmware presses a key, waits for a light change, deletes, repeats; random jitter between samples avoids locking to refresh ([Hume](https://thume.ca/2020/05/20/making-a-latency-tester/)). Molodetskikh's variant sends keys from the board itself (so keyboard firmware is excluded), 120 repetitions per test, about 35,500 samples/s ([bxt.rs](https://bxt.rs/blog/just-how-much-faster-are-the-gnome-46-terminals/)).
- Sensor position matters: about 16 ms difference between top and bottom of a 60 Hz panel due to row scan-out ([Hume](https://thume.ca/2020/05/20/making-a-latency-tester/)).
- Dan Luu used 10k keypresses per measurement and reported p50 and p99.9, idle and under load, and found window size noticeably affects results ([Dan Luu](https://danluu.com/term-latency/)).
- zwp_input_timestamps_v1 gives nanosecond-resolution versions of input event timestamps "in the same clock domain" as the core event timestamp; the protocol does not name the clock ([wayland.app, input-timestamps](https://wayland.app/protocols/input-timestamps-unstable-v1)). wp_presentation's clock is announced at bind and is explicitly not guaranteed to be the input timestamp clock ([wayland.app, presentation-time](https://wayland.app/protocols/presentation-time)).
- Desktop compositor and editor work is visible in system-wide latency: a 2026 click-to-photon study on KWin reported an idle Zed instance adding up to 3 ms system-wide input latency, and black frame insertion adding a full frame (8.3 ms at 120 Hz) ([farnoy.dev via fooqux](https://fooqux.com/article/5675)). Aggregated copy; original at farnoy.dev returned 404.

### Inferences
- The wtype problem: wtype uses the zwp_virtual_keyboard protocol, so the compositor synthesises the key event and its `time` field is whatever the compositor or client supplies, not a kernel evdev time. Core `wl_keyboard.key` time is milliseconds with an undefined base (Wayland core protocol), so it cannot be subtracted from a wp_presentation timestamp without knowing the compositor's clock. Recommendation: inject with a small uinput program that records `clock_gettime(CLOCK_MONOTONIC)` immediately before `write()` of the EV_KEY/SYN_REPORT, and in the client record CLOCK_MONOTONIC on receipt plus the wp_presentation timestamp (check that `clock_id` is CLOCK_MONOTONIC, which it is on wlroots and Mutter in practice; verify by logging it). evdev stamps events with the kernel's event clock (settable via EVIOCSCLOCKID; libinput uses CLOCK_MONOTONIC), so uinput gives real kernel-time starts. ydotool also uses uinput and is usable if you log your own send time. These clock statements are from general knowledge and were not verified against a fetched source this session.
- Three-layer measurement for vimmed:
  1. Hand-off: key receipt (client, CLOCK_MONOTONIC) to `wl_surface.commit` / CATransaction flush. Cheap, every CI run.
  2. On-glass estimate: uinput send time to wp_presentation `presented` (record `flags`; only trust samples with `vsync` and ideally `hw_clock`/`hw_completion`; drop `discarded`). Log `refresh` so results can be normalised to refresh periods.
  3. Photodiode: Teensy-style tester for a periodic calibration run, sensor at a fixed screen row, to measure what wp_presentation omits (scan-out row offset, panel response, and on real keyboards the keyboard). The spike's manual typing runs are a weak substitute; a 240 fps phone camera gives about 4.2 ms resolution, useful only for sanity checks.
- Statistics: report p50, p95, p99 and max with sample count. For p95 with a reasonable confidence interval, collect at least several hundred samples per condition (Molodetskikh used 120 per test, Dan Luu 10k); randomise inter-key intervals (for example 100 to 250 ms uniform) so the phase relative to vblank is uniformly sampled, otherwise percentiles are artefacts. Separate idle and loaded runs (Dan Luu showed tails blow out under load). Fix CPU governor and power mode (foot's wiki noted variation in battery mode).
- Baseline on the same machine and compositor in the same session: foot + neovim (and optionally Alacritty), same window size and font size, measured with the same uinput rig and, where possible, presentation feedback via a wrapper. Without the baseline the absolute number is not interpretable.

### Gaps
- No source found documenting foot's or kitty's own in-tree latency measurement tooling beyond render timers and Typometer/hardware references.
- No source found for is-it-snappy methodology details (not fetched).
- Exact clock used by Hyprland for wp_presentation and for key event timestamps was not verified.

## 5. A sensible, defensible latency target for vimmed

### Takeaway
Define the target in two layers: an app-controlled hand-off budget expressed in milliseconds, and an on-glass budget expressed in refresh periods and relative to a terminal-vim baseline on the same machine. The proposed redefinition is right in direction but should keep a hard on-glass bound so hand-off cannot be gamed by pushing the wait into the compositor.

### Cited Findings
- Perception research: Ng et al. reported perception limits of 2 ms for dragging and 6 ms for scribbling on direct-touch; Deber et al. (CHI 2015) found users detect dragging latency as low as 6 ms; Jota et al. (CHI 2013) found task performance decreases as latency increases ([Deber, Jota, Forlines, Wigdor, CHI 2015 PDF](https://dcmpx.remotevs.com/com/tactuallabs/www/SL/papers/howMuchFasterIsFastEnoughCHI15.pdf), [Jota et al. 2013, researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/JotaNDW13)). These are direct-touch results; indirect keyboard input tolerates more, but they show that "faster is noticeable" extends well below one frame.
- GNOME HIG cited a 10 ms threshold that no modern terminal met in 2018 Typometer tests ([LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/751763/)).
- Fatin's target for editor processing: 1 to 5 ms; 16 ms called arbitrary ([Fatin](https://pavelfatin.com/typing-with-pleasure/)).
- The best editors and terminals measured end to end at 60 Hz sit around 33 to 36 ms average ([Hume](https://thume.ca/2020/05/20/making-a-latency-tester/)).

### Inferences
Recommended spec wording (proposal, derived from the above):
1. **Hand-off (app budget):** key event received by the client to frame committed to the compositor (wl_surface.commit with attached buffer, or CATransaction flush): p95 <= 8 ms and p99 <= 16 ms on the reference Linux machine, idle, typical buffer. Rationale: 8 ms is half a 60 Hz frame and leaves headroom for late-latching compositors; the raw Wayland shell (5 ms) passes, the GTK4 shell (14 ms) does not, which is the correct signal because at 60 Hz 14 ms routinely misses a late latch. If 16 ms is kept for continuity, keep it as p99, not p95.
   - Caveat: when the toolkit waits on the frame callback before drawing (GTK4), measure hand-off from "frame callback received" as well, so frame-clock waiting is not counted as app work, or measure the app's render time separately.
2. **On-glass (system budget, recorded and gated):** uinput key send to wp_presentation `presented`, normalised by `refresh`: p50 <= 1.75 refresh periods and p95 <= 2.5 refresh periods (60 Hz: about 29 ms and 42 ms; 120 Hz: about 15 ms and 21 ms; 144 Hz: about 12 and 17 ms). These reflect the random-phase floor model (commit to presented uniform on [T, 2T] for an early-compositing compositor) plus a small app margin; the spike's 28 to 31 / 40 ms at 60 Hz sits at the edge, which is honest.
3. **Relative to baseline (the real "vim, but rendered" promise):** on the same machine, compositor and session, vimmed on-glass p50 and p95 must be no worse than foot + neovim by more than 0.25 refresh periods (about 4 ms at 60 Hz), and never one full refresh worse at p50. This is what users will actually feel and it is robust to compositor and hardware changes.
4. **Report, do not gate:** photodiode key-to-light on the calibration rig, loaded-system runs (for example during a cargo build), and macOS ProMotion glass times via Metal/Core Animation presented timestamps.
5. **Method requirements in the spec:** uinput injection with CLOCK_MONOTONIC send time; at least 500 samples per condition with randomised 100 to 250 ms gaps; fixed window size, font and file; report p50/p95/p99/max and n; reject samples whose presentation flags lack `vsync`, and record whether `hw_completion` was set.

Why not "glass p95 <= 16 ms at 60 Hz": impossible for any vsynced composited client, since the random-phase wait alone is about 0.95 of a frame at p95. Why not hand-off only: a client that commits immediately and a client that waits on frame callbacks give identical glass times (as the spike showed), so hand-off alone rewards nothing the user perceives. The pair (hand-off ms plus glass in refresh periods plus relative-to-foot) is defensible.

### Gaps
- No published standard or industry consensus target for editor key-to-glass in refresh-period units was found; the numbers above are derived proposals, not sourced thresholds.
- Perception thresholds specifically for keyboard typing with visual echo (as opposed to direct touch) were not found in a primary source this session.
