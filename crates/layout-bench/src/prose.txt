Most mornings the river fog settled over the valley before sunrise, and the farmers waited for it to lift before starting their tractors. By eight o'clock the paddocks were usually clear, although the shaded gullies kept their mist until well after lunch. Visitors often remarked that the district looked unchanged from old photographs, yet almost every family had quietly rebuilt their sheds, replaced their fences and planted different crops over the previous twenty years. The general store still opened at seven, selling newspapers, bread, fishing bait and whatever spare parts the owner remembered to order. Children walked along the gravel road to catch the school bus, carrying lunches wrapped in paper and arguing about football results. In winter the evenings arrived early, and kitchen windows glowed across the hills while dinner simmered slowly on wood stoves.
